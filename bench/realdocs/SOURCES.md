# Real-document recordings: sources and licences

The recordings in [`recordings/`](recordings/) are not the documents. For each document
they hold the text Intern's parser worker extracted from it (every page's text and its
layout blocks, compacted), the SHA-256 of each prompt the engine built from that text, and
the model's reply with its token probabilities. The documents themselves are never
committed: [`scripts/realdocs-eval/fetch.py`](../../scripts/realdocs-eval/fetch.py) fetches
them from their sources, which the samples in `scripts/realdocs-eval/` pin by size and
SHA-256.

## The rule

A document's recorded text is committed only when its source licence plainly permits
redistribution:

- public domain: a work of the US federal government (17 U.S.C. 105), CC0, a Public Domain
  Mark or a public-domain dedication;
- the UK Open Government Licence v3.0;
- a Creative Commons Attribution licence, any version, without NC or ND.

Everything else is left out: public records with no licence text, documents with no explicit
licence or an explicit "not CC-licensed", site terms of use (ACER, the London Datastore),
other licences (the FreeBSD Documentation License, the British Columbia and Canada open
government licences), library rights statements such as "no known copyright restrictions",
and anything unclear. A licence, mark or dedication covers only what the party giving it could
license, so a document that the licence statement or the source says someone else wrote is
left out too: a letter a company or its counsel wrote that an agency published, a supplier's
invoice in an FOI release (OGL v3.0 does not cover third-party rights), a Gazette notice a
company placed, an invoice an uploader rather than its author dedicated to the public domain,
a company's record in a data set that someone else compiled and licensed (the Enron records in
the EDRM data set: ZL Technologies licensed its compilation under CC BY 3.0 US, and SheetJS its
repackaging under CC0, but neither wrote the records). So is a document that encloses or
reproduces such writing in full: an OIA response with the suppliers' invoices behind its card
transactions appended, a FOIA response enclosing a company's letters and memos and a travel
agency's itinerary, a penalty notice that appends the company's representations. An
employee's own writing in an agency's release is someone else's too, unless it was part of their
official duties (17 U.S.C. 105 covers only that): a FOIA response releasing the forms in which an
employee applied to hold a board seat in a personal capacity is left out. A quotation within an
agency's own reasoning is part of the agency's text.

A Public Domain Mark says that a work is free of copyright; it is not a dedication by whoever
holds the rights. One that a record's holder, not its author, applied is accepted only for a
firm's bill or a public body's notice made before 1906. In the US an unpublished work made for
hire, or one that names no person as its author, is protected for at most 120 years from its
making (17 U.S.C. 302(c), 303(a)), so a bill a firm's clerk made out, or a notice a public body
issued, is out of copyright that old whoever marked it. An unpublished work a named person wrote
is protected for that person's life and 70 years (302(a), 303(a)) however old it is: a holder's
mark never clears a person's own letters or papers. The invoices of 1858 to 1898 are firms'
printed billheads filled in for the firm; one that a proprietor or partner made out himself
would still be protected only if he lived past 1956, 58 years after the latest of them, and the
one bill in a single proprietor's name, George P. McNear's of 1895, is not (he died in 1947).
The notice of 1884 is a county's printed order, signed by the Local Authority's clerk as its
clerk. They are cleared on that ground; the invoices of 1916, 1920 and 1923 are not. A
dedication counts when the rights holder made it: Public.Resource.Org's of its own letters, and
the dedication of the Wright brothers' unpublished writings that the Library of Congress
reports.
The classification reads each document's licence statement in its sample (`source.license`),
and was decided for this round by the maintainer's rule above (November 2026).

Every committed document was changed in the sense the CC BY licences ask to be told: what is
committed is text extracted from it by software, not the document. The attribution each
licence requires is given per document below.

A table row starting with a document id is a cleared document; `Recorded` says whether the
committed recording holds it (`scripts/realdocs-gate.test.ts` checks both lists against the
recordings and the baselines). A cleared document not in the recording was never recorded:
its fetch failed on the runner. Excluded documents are listed with the reason.
